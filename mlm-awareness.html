MLM Awareness

Before joining any multi-level marketing or network-marketing opportunity, understand the business model, compensation structure, product demand, recruitment requirements and financial mathematics.

THINK BEFORE YOU JOIN

An opportunity deserves research before commitment.

MLM is not automatically a scam, Ponzi scheme or pyramid scheme. Different business models can have very different structures. The purpose of this page is not to attack legitimate businesses or people who participate in them. The objective is simple: understand the structure before putting in money, time, relationships or personal information.

A useful question: If recruitment stopped tomorrow, would the business still have strong enough genuine customer demand for its products or services to continue operating?
STEP 1

Research the structure — not just the opportunity.

🔎

Investigate the company

Look for corporate information, management, product information, financial disclosures where available, regulatory information, complaints, litigation and independent reporting.

📊

Understand the compensation plan

Don't rely only on motivational presentations. Understand exactly how commissions are generated, what activities qualify for payment and whether recruitment or purchases are required.

🧮

Do the mathematics

Ask how many customers are required, how many distributors are required and how the structure behaves when recruitment becomes increasingly difficult.

BUSINESS MODEL

Product demand vs recruitment dependency

A useful way to analyse an opportunity is to examine where the economic activity actually comes from.

Question Product-driven model Recruitment-dependent model
Where does revenue primarily come from? Sales of products/services to customers. May depend heavily on distributor recruitment, qualifying purchases or internal consumption.
What happens if recruitment stops? Customer demand can continue generating revenue. Growth and commission opportunities may become increasingly constrained.
Who is the customer? A genuine end consumer with a reason to purchase. Potentially another participant or distributor.
What should you investigate? Product demand, competition, pricing and margins. Recruitment requirements, incentives and compensation structure.
Important: This table is an analytical framework, not a legal test for determining whether a particular company is legitimate or illegal.
THE MATHEMATICS

Why recruitment-based growth becomes difficult

If every participant is expected to recruit several additional participants, the number of people required grows exponentially. Consider a simplified example where every participant must recruit 3 people.

Level New participants Total participants
1 3 3
2 9 12
3 27 39
4 81 120
5 243 363
6 729 1,092
10 59,049 88,572
The mathematics does not prove that a particular company is a Ponzi scheme. It demonstrates why a recruitment-dependent model faces a structural growth problem when the available population of recruits is finite.

BEHAVIOURAL AWARENESS

Recognising escalating red flags

One isolated behaviour does not prove that an organisation is fraudulent. However, a combination of pressure, isolation, financial escalation and recruitment dependency deserves careful examination.

Stage 1 — Attention

Early enthusiasm and social pressure

  • Unusually intense attention toward a new participant.
  • Large success stories are presented early.
  • The opportunity is presented as an exceptional chance.
  • Questions are redirected toward motivational messaging.
Stage 2 — Commitment

Increasing financial or emotional commitment

  • Pressure to make decisions quickly.
  • Joining fees or purchases become normalised.
  • Participants are encouraged to attend more meetings.
  • Outside criticism may be dismissed as negativity.
Stage 3 — Escalation

The cost of staying involved increases

  • Purchases, subscriptions or recurring commitments increase.
  • Participants are encouraged to recruit others to recover previous expenses.
  • Income claims focus heavily on exceptional success stories.
  • The number of participants appears to grow faster than genuine external customer demand.
  • Leaving becomes psychologically or financially difficult.
  • Participants are encouraged to make increasingly large commitments because they have already invested heavily.
Stage 4 — Stop & independently verify

Create distance before deciding

At this point, stop relying exclusively on the people recruiting you.

  • Read the official compensation plan yourself.
  • Calculate realistic expenses and income.
  • Speak to people who left.
  • Look for independent evidence.
  • Check regulatory and legal information where relevant.
  • Take time before making another payment.
  • Never let urgency replace due diligence.
DECISION CHECKLIST

Ask these questions before joining

1. Customers

Who is actually buying the product or service outside the distributor network?

2. Revenue

Can I understand exactly how the company generates revenue?

3. Compensation

Can I independently calculate how commissions are earned?

4. Costs

What are all the upfront, recurring and hidden costs?

5. Recruitment

Would my income depend significantly on recruiting other people?

6. Evidence

What independent evidence supports the income claims being presented?
